It's 2026, and producing code is easy. So easy that people who have never coded before build small apps for their own use with $20/month agents. A huge app is still hard to build and even harder to distribute, but it's no longer impossible to build solo. Actually, it's never been impossible. Only a few people happened to do it (RIP Terry Davis).

Watching people vibecode Photoshop and give it away under the MIT license, you'd have to be crazy to think you can still gatekeep your app behind a paywall and that nobody will be smart enough to rebuild it.

So what are your options? In 2026, the only one is to open-source your app. That doesn't guarantee users, but it does buy some trust. To get users, you have to grind a lot on social media and create content that builds a following around the app, or the problem it solves, and the founder being a nice guy.

With that said, in 2026 code becomes content, GitHub becomes YouTube, the builder becomes an influencer, and the product becomes a community around nice solutions to well-researched problems. This is a form of interaction.

There will still be companies that build massive software with investor money, just like Sony and Paramount still make movies with expensive production, and those companies are ai labs.

I'm not an ai lab. That's why I decided to open-source [Starquix](https://starquix.app/) and talk publicly to myself. You might want to [contribute](https://github.com/Mayhopar/Starquix) or [join the Discord](https://discord.gg/Fpuwzbd48V) to make sure I talk to others too.
