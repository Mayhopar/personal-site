It’s October 2026, and I just created a blog and am writing a first post. This is my first official tech-bro style blog and post. What made me so shameless that all of a sudden I can afford talking to myself publicly?

A current state of the internet. I’m just very bored to be online. And since online is everything that I know, it’s easier for me to build a blog than to go buy a notebook and remember how to use a pen.

So dear diary, hear me out. Internet used to be a place with cool people who had their personal websites and blogs, and oftentimes after interacting with them you’d feel an increment on your mood or knowledge about something. Paul Graham’s or Sheldon Brown’s websites are great examples of this. But back when I was a teenager, I’d read some people’s blogs and learn about culture mostly.

Then social media arrived and it was also very freaking cool at the beginning. Especially in Ukraine, where we had our own Russian Facebook by Pavel Durov, which had all the music and films and shows for free. Up to this point it was elevating your life by giving you access to people you would love to interact with (or just love – I met my wife on [are.na](http://are.na)). And from there things degraded to doomscrolls of Instagram, X, Reddit etc.

Think about it for a second.

Internet is now a tool to consume images and videos on a loop for the majority of people. The whole internet is just a few platforms where algorithms [decide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PpcP835mWEQ) what I would engage with Bombardilo Crocodilo. Maybe only TikTok stands out, because there I would actually find interesting people who would train their LLM with a potato. But in order to find that pearl I’d need to make a very long dive in the sea of shit.

Another aspect is that my laptop now is doing the work for me all the time. I just need to wire some money to Dario and give Claude a prompt. So there are no multi-hour flow states anymore to produce work that an LLM now produces for me in a few minutes. I also do not need to learn as hard and think as hard in a moment. I have these huge pauses in between focused “work” and inability to consume social media makes this experience unbearable.

The other day I was walking in the neighborhood and saw a Spike’s magazine office. I know this name since the times of cool internet. I knew it’s some kind of art magazine, and that’s it. So I decided to check their website because I support my homies. There was an [article](https://spikeartmagazine.com/articles/libra-season-what-criticism-can-do) about New York, an artist who died, and a restaurant he opened but closed a few days before he died. Super random stuff, but it felt very gonzo to read it, and actually good. Just like it was when the internet was young and carried that energy of randomness in it.

And then I thought what is the more human thing in the dead internet time of today, when Claude is sweating it’s ass off to deliver your million dollar SaaS idea? Kick back and gather your random thoughts together and maybe share them with someone in a blog. Maybe I fell a victim of a neo-luddite tyranny of Berlin, very much can be, but I also assume I may not be so unique to have this feeling.

So, a useful conclusion. Blogs feel kinda cool again. It’s a very human and naive activity. You just live your life, have some experiences, share them, bots read it and train a new model. Profit.
